How Hell works

The Rules of Hell

Hell is repetition. Everyone down there does the same thing forever. Everything in the game, from rewind to dying, comes from that one rule.

Déjà Vu

Hell runs everything on a loop. The hero grabs the loop and pulls it backward: hold R (or the ◀◀ button) and the last few seconds run in reverse, monsters, shots and all. Then the same future plays again, the same way, unless he does something different.

FuelThe Déjà Vu meter
RefillLoop shards, from the damned
LockedWhen something bigger has hold of the loop

He finds out how it works on the Loop, Floor 995, where Hell visibly repeats: the same stones sinking in the same order, the same geyser at the same spot, the same cinder drifting in at the same moment every lap, and YOU ARE HERE. AGAIN. carved into the rock three times.

The heroIt's doing that on purpose.

The heroHell runs on a loop. So I can drag it back.

The damned are souls stuck in Hell's loop, repeating one floor forever: climbing the same three steps, counting the same stones. They don't fight. When the hero passes close, his loop brushes theirs, theirs hiccups, and a loop shard drops onto the ledge. Then they go straight back to what they were doing.

In a scene, something bigger has hold of the loop (the portal's pull, a Warden's entrance), so he can't grab it. The button just says Not now.

Grudges

Every floor gives the hero something new to be annoyed about, and spite is fuel. On the landing of every fifth floor, and after every floor boss, he picks a Grudge: Spite Rounds, Short Fuse, Not Today, Déjà Vu. Grudges are the run's. When Hell ends the run, they go.

The village's diamonds

Diamonds are what holds a portal open. That's why the Overlord stole them, and why he's feeding them into his gate at Floor 1. In Hell they come back from whoever was carrying them: sack imps spill them, the floor bosses sit on piles of them, demons have them stuck in their rock, the Magma King has his cut. Rewards make sense, or they aren't rewards.

Yooba's Goods

Diamonds go to Yooba, who works them into the hero's gear between floors. What he makes is real (slime in the boots, a licked trigger, a loop shard in a jar), so it stays when a run ends. Hell can take spite. It can't take boots.

Waystones and runs

Hell doesn't let you die. It sends you back to repeat the floors. A run is one climb from a Waystone until Hell kills him. Waystones are the stones where Hell starts its loops over: the Landing at Floor 1,000, then the top of every Circle once its Warden is beaten (901 when the Magma King falls, then 801, and so on).

Die on a floor of Hell and the death screen says YOU OBVIOUSLY DIED. Then the Shaft: the marker sits on the floor he died on, then falls, faster and faster, back to the highest Waystone and lands with a slam. The Grudges are gone. The diamonds and Yooba's goods stay.

The heroOf course it does.

Shortcuts

Hell is repetition, so it hates a shortcut, and it leaves them lying around anyway: a stairwell that fell in, an elevator nobody trusts, a demon who owes you for sparing it. A shortcut skips a whole stretch of floors. It's a choice: keep climbing the long way (and play every floor it skips), or take it and pay its cost. The card says what the shortcut is and what it costs. It never says how far it goes. The choice is final: Déjà Vu can't undo it.

The first one is the Old Stair at Floor 871, a collapsed stairwell carved STAIRS (CLOSED). It costs a Grudge.
